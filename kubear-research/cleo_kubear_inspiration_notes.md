# Cleo and Kubear public-surface notes

## Cleo public marketing surface

The reviewed Cleo homepage opens with a compact, personality-led statement, then immediately offers a direct product trial. Its long scroll is built as a sequence of high-energy statements, large animation-led visual scenes, product moments, and clear calls to action. The strongest transferable ideas are **one dominant characterful point of view**, a visual system that changes scene by scene, motion that explains a change rather than merely decorating the page, and an app-download CTA that appears before and after the narrative.

Kubear should not reproduce Cleo’s highly confrontational “talks back” persona, purple/blue brand world, copy, character relationship, US banking claims, or its animation assets. Kubear’s equivalent should be a warm, direct Indian money companion. Instead of “being called out” for spending, the emotional payoff is seeing salary, UPI, bills, SIP and home expenses click into one clear view. The motion should be satisfying and responsive, not chaotic or scolding.

## Kubear web app entry surface

The inspected `https://kubear.kuberos.in` app entry loaded with the title “Kubear - Money, Managed” but showed no rendered interactive content in the audit browser. The marketing site should keep this URL as the primary “Open Kubear” route, but avoid asserting unverified onboarding or app-interface details until product screenshots or a working public entry state are supplied.

## Kubear Play Store surface

The provided Google Play listing describes Kubear as a personal and household money manager for adults in India. Its approved listing copy says that users can record income and spending, plan how salary covers monthly needs, keep bills and goals visible, understand what remains after planned commitments, and coordinate selected shared household entries while keeping personal entries separate. It also says Kubear does not hold or move money and is not a bank, wallet, lender, broker, insurer, tax service, investment adviser or legal adviser. The listing currently presents finance category, 0+ downloads, and an initial-release update dated 4 August 2026. These facts are suitable for cautious marketing claims, but the 0+ download count must not be used as social proof.

The Google Play gallery establishes a useful, owned app-art direction: bold editorial phrases, deep ink or saturated fields, high-contrast cream/orange copy, composed app-screen cut-outs, and a recurring small orange signal. Headlines include variations on “Your money. Your rules.”, “Your money. No small talk.”, “Protect the plan. Share on your terms.” and “Your goals called. Ready when you are.” The next marketing-site iteration can translate this into an original web story without copying the exact layouts or wording. The direct Android CTA is `https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=in.kuberos.kubear&pcampaignid=web_share`.

## Research and brand-reference leads

The next plan will draw product-story implications from credible research rather than turning anecdotal finance behavior into unsupported marketing statistics. Current leads include an Indian UPI-spending survey published on arXiv, RBI annual-report payment statistics, and RBI/PIB financial-inclusion reporting. The site will use familiar situations such as a Goa trip fund, rent, a card bill, UPI spending and a shared household plan as illustrations, clearly labelled as examples rather than claims about a visitor.

The user also asked about references to brands such as Zomato. The plan will separate ordinary nominative text references from brand logos, endorsements, copied product imagery and misleading affiliation. The next implementation recommendation will use either generic terms like “food delivery” or legally reviewed context-only textual references, with no third-party logos or product screenshots by default.

## Planned content policy

Use concrete Indian money situations to make the visual story recognisable. “Goa trip fund”, “Friday card bill”, “shared rent”, “school fees” and “food delivery” can all appear as example ledger labels. If a specific service name such as Zomato is used, it must be plain text in a proportionate, factual example and must not appear with a logo, product UI, affiliation claim or a visual treatment likely to confuse users about a relationship. Final public third-party-brand language requires counsel review.
